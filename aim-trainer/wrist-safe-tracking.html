Wrist-Safe Tracking

Smooth tracking and healthy wrists want the same thing: a relaxed arm doing the big movement, a straight wrist doing the small corrections, and short sessions with real rest. Set up the posture below once, then use the timeline every time you train on Tracking Ladder.

How to sit

Side view

The green strip is where your arm takes its weight: the fleshy part of the forearm. Leaning on the wrist crease or a hard desk edge squeezes the carpal tunnel, and leaning on the inside of the elbow presses on the nerve that runs to your little finger.

At 38.3 cm per 360°, 1 cm of mouse turns you 9.4°, and one edge-to-edge swipe of your 45 cm pad is a full 360°. An Overwatch hero strafing at full speed 10 m away moves about 31°/s, which is only about 3.3 cm of mouse per second. Tier 12 of the ladder is 70°/s, about 7.5 cm/s. Tracking is slow, steady travel, so let the forearm carry it and save the wrist for small corrections. A wrist that does all the travel ends up bent at the extremes, which is where strain builds.

  1. Warm-up: tendon glides and two easy runs one tier below your current one. Cold tendons strain more easily.
  2. Rest about as long as each 30-second run. Let go of the mouse and shake out your hand between runs.
  3. Check your grip every few runs. Squeeze only hard enough to lift the mouse; a tight grip makes tracking jittery and tires the forearm.
  4. Stop after 20 minutes even on a good day. Gains come from daily repetition and sleep, not from long sessions.
  5. When you play Overwatch afterwards, stand up and move for 5 minutes each hour.

Before: tendon glides

  • Five hand shapesOpen hand, hook fist, full fist, flat fist (knuckles straight), straight fist. Move slowly through them.
    5 rounds · 1 min
  • Wrist circlesArms out, slow circles both ways. Small range, no force.
    10 each way

After: gentle stretches

  • Forearm top (extensors)Arm straight, palm down. Use the other hand to bend the wrist down until you feel a mild pull.
    20–30 s · each arm
  • Forearm underside (flexors)Arm straight, palm up. Ease the fingers back toward you.
    20–30 s · each arm
  • Neck and shouldersShoulder rolls, then tilt each ear toward its shoulder.
    30 s

Stop and rest if

  • Tingling or numbness in the thumb, index or middle fingerPressure on the median nerve at the wrist. Stop, and check you aren't resting on the wrist or bending it back.
  • Tingling in the ring or little fingerUsually leaning on the elbow or the desk edge. Move your support to the forearm.
  • Pain still there the next day, or weaknessTake a few days off and see a physiotherapist or doctor if it doesn't settle.

General ergonomics guidance, not medical advice. The 15° figure reflects lab work such as Keir, Bach and Rempel (1999), which measured carpal tunnel pressure rising as the wrist bends back during mouse use. Angles and distances on this page use your settings: 1600 DPI, Overwatch sens 2.26.
